  The direct buffer test: has the inbound delay exceeded the scheduled ground time?
    inbound delay EXCEEDS the scheduled ground time  n= 41,495  P(y)= 93.5%  P(y_prop)= 77.1%

  Reading: the outcome rises monotonically with inbound delay and falls monotonically
  with the scheduled ground time available to absorb it, which is the behaviour a
  buffer-absorption account predicts and a generic 'departures are sometimes late'
  account does not.

  IS THE MODEL THEREFORE JUST DOING ARITHMETIC?
  In the cells where the inbound delay already exceeds the scheduled ground time the
  outcome is close to deterministic, so a fair objection is that the task is trivial.
  It is not, because that regime is a small minority of the data. Restricting to the
  turnarounds where the inbound delay still fits inside the scheduled ground time:
    full test set                 : n=128,450  prevalence 28.08%  AUC 0.887  AUPRC 0.851
    inbound delay fits the buffer : n=116,837  prevalence 21.46%  AUC 0.838  AUPRC 0.732
    that subset is 91.0% of the test data, so the model is doing real
    discrimination on the great majority of turnarounds, not arithmetic on a minority.
