VALIDATION AGAINST OPERATIONAL CONDITIONS (reviewer item 1, third element)
  The referee asked for the outcome to be validated against delay causes, schedule
  buffers AND operational conditions. A fair objection to the buffer-absorption
  evidence is that it might simply track disrupted days: on a bad-weather or
  high-ATFM day everything is late, inbound and outbound alike, and the apparent
  buffer mechanism would be spurious. We therefore build a daily disruption index
  from the BTS causal fields and test whether the relationship survives within strata.

  airport-days with at least 20 attributed departures; disruption tertiles at 0.42 and 0.53

  stratum          n  disruption  P(y) exceeds  P(y) fits   ratio     AUC
  low        143,937        0.35         93.5%      19.6%     4.8   0.889
  medium     143,802        0.48         93.4%      19.7%     4.8   0.877
  high       143,802        0.63         92.9%      21.9%     4.3   0.882

  The buffer relationship holds with a similar ratio inside every disruption stratum,
  and discrimination is stable across them, so the outcome is not merely tracking
  disrupted days. Overall prevalence does rise with disruption, which is expected and
  is exactly the base-rate shift that the per-airport calibration analysis addresses.
